# Decision memo: where the webinar loses its audience

**To:** Head of Marketing, webinar presenters · **From:** Analytics · **Data:** synthetic demo, last 45 days

> Figures are from the live demo on the day this memo was written. The [live executive summary](https://nikhilveerwani.github.io/webinar-audience-console/) recomputes them every morning.

## The decision

The webinar is the main top-of-funnel event. Should we change how the session is structured, and if so, where?

## What we found (Regular webinars, n ≈ 43)

1. **The room peaks around minute 7, then loses most people in one window: minute 34–49.** That 15 minutes costs about 30 points of the peak, more than any other 15 minutes in the session.
2. **Qualified attendees stay much longer:** about 70% of peak at +45 against 44% for everyone else. The gap is about 26 points (95% CI 23–29, paired across webinars, p < 0.001), so it's consistent session after session, not a few outliers.
3. **But qualified attendees' steepest drop is in the same window (minute 35–50).** So the drop is not just the wrong audience filtering itself out. Something at that point in the session loses everyone, including the people we most want to keep.

## Options

| Option | Upside | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| A. Leave the session as it is | No effort | Keeps losing a third of the room, including qualified people, before the offer lands |
| B. Rewrite the whole session | Could fix more than one issue | Many changes at once: we won't know what worked |
| **C. Move one thing and test it (recommended)** | Isolates the cause; cheap to run | Takes 4 weeks |

## Recommendation: Option C

1. **Move the offer and the main Q&A before minute 34** in alternate sessions for 4 weeks. Keep everything else the same.
2. **Judge on two numbers:** qualified retention at +45, and sales from attendees within 30 days. Retention alone could rise while sales fall, if the offer lands too early.
3. **Meanwhile, follow up within the hour with qualified attendees who leave before minute 49.** They showed intent by registering and attending; they're the warmest list we have.

## What would change this recommendation

- The leave-time data shows most leavers in that window had **already bought** or booked a call. Then the drop is success, not loss, and the session should stay as it is.
- The A/B shows retention rising but attendee sales flat: then the issue is the offer itself, not where it sits.
